# BUSN31150-Final-Project

This project uses DHS and World Bank data to examine the relationship between water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions and child health outcomes across developing countries. Through data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, SQL queries, and regression modeling, we identify the WASH indicators most strongly associated with u5_mortality, infant_mortality, and diarrhea_rate. We then construct a WASH Investment Urgency Index to rank countries based on WASH deficits and potential health gains from intervention. The results show that improved water access is the strongest predictor of lower child mortality and that the highest-priority countries are concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa, providing a data-driven framework for policymakers and development organizations to target future WASH investments.
